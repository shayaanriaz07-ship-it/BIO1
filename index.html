Study this first

This is pulled directly from the Unit 1 review slides. If time is short, know every bold idea below before going into the broader flashcard deck. The exam is about 60–80 questions, and Chapters 3–4 make up about 65% of it.

Chapter 1 — about 15%

  • Be able to identify: independent variable, dependent variable, controlled variables, experimental group, and control group from a scenario.
  • Quantitative = numbers/measurements. Qualitative = descriptions such as leaf color or wilting.
  • Hypothesis = testable and falsifiable; scientific theory = broad explanation backed by lots of evidence and repeated testing.

Chapter 2 — about 20%

  • Atom math: atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons; neutrons = mass number − atomic number; a neutral atom has electrons = protons.
  • Know ion vs. isotope; covalent (share electrons) vs. ionic (electron transfer then attraction) vs. hydrogen bonds.
  • Water and pH: lower pH means more acidic; buffers resist sudden pH changes; water polarity allows hydrogen bonding, cohesion/adhesion, dissolving polar/ionic substances, and temperature buffering.

Chapter 3 — about 30%

  • Dehydration synthesis removes water to join monomers; hydrolysis adds water to break polymers.
  • Know the macromolecule chart: building blocks, functions, and examples for carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.
  • Protein shape controls function: heat or pH can denature a protein, changing its 3-D shape/active site.
  • Starch vs. cellulose: both are glucose polymers, but different linkages give different shapes; iodine tests positive for starch, not cellulose.
  • Saturated fat: no C=C bonds, straighter, often solid. Unsaturated: one or more C=C bonds, kinked, often liquid.

Chapter 4 — about 35%

  • Prokaryote vs. eukaryote: both have DNA, cytoplasm, plasma membrane, and ribosomes. Only eukaryotes have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
  • Organelle functions: mitochondrion = ATP; Golgi = modifies/packages; ribosome = protein synthesis; smooth ER = lipids; nucleus = chromosomes; nucleolus = ribosome assembly; lysosome = recycling; chloroplast = photosynthesis; central vacuole = plant water storage.
  • Secreted-protein path: ribosome → rough ER → Golgi → vesicle → plasma membrane.
  • Cell size problem: volume grows faster than surface area, making exchange less efficient. Phospholipid bilayer: heads face water, tails face inward.

Worked examples from the study guide

A researcher tests whether fertilizer concentration affects bean plant growth — identify the IV, DV, controlled variables, and control group.

IV = fertilizer concentration. DV = plant height/growth after 3 weeks. Controlled variables = things like species, starting size, water, soil, light, temperature, pot size, experiment length. Control group = the plants receiving no fertilizer (baseline).

Plant masses recorded as 5.2 g, 6.1 g, 4.8 g, 7.0 g, 5.7 g — quantitative or qualitative?

Quantitative — the observations are numerical measurements.

What would qualitative data about those same plants look like?

Leaf color, wilting, visible disease symptoms — descriptive, non-numerical observations.

Worked examples from the study guide

An atom has atomic number 8 and mass number 16 — how many protons and neutrons?

8 protons, 8 neutrons (mass number − atomic number = neutrons).

If that same atom is electrically neutral, how many electrons does it have?

8 electrons — electrons equal protons in a neutral atom.

A solution changes from pH 7 to pH 4 — more acidic or more basic?

More acidic. Lower pH = more H⁺ = more acidic.

Water molecules attract each other because a slightly positive region of one interacts with a slightly negative region of another — what interaction is this?

Hydrogen bonding.

Why does water's polarity matter biologically?

It lets water form hydrogen bonds, makes water a great solvent for ions/polar substances, drives cohesion/adhesion, buffers temperature, and supports many biological reactions.

Worked examples from the study guide

Joining monomers together while removing water is called ______.

Dehydration synthesis (condensation reaction).

Breaking a polymer apart by adding water is called ______.

Hydrolysis.

A protein is exposed to very high heat and loses its 3-D shape — what happened?

Denaturation.

A substance has glycerol attached to three fatty acids — what type of molecule is it?

A lipid — specifically a triglyceride.

Starch and cellulose are both made of glucose — why does only starch test positive with iodine?

Their glucose units are linked differently. Iodine reacts with starch's helical structure (especially amylose) to give the color change; cellulose's β-glucose linkages form straight chains that don't react the same way.

Notes she flagged: An enzyme stops working after denaturation because its shape/active site changes so it can no longer bind its substrate — the peptide bonds themselves usually stay intact. Cellulose IS a carbohydrate (a polysaccharide) even though humans can't digest it. Vegetable oil is liquid while butter is solid because double bonds create kinks that keep unsaturated fat chains from packing tightly. Monounsaturated = one double bond; polyunsaturated = more than one.
Chapter 4 — Cell Structure ~35% +

What she's prioritizing

  • Prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic
  • Cell size limits
  • Membrane structure
  • Organelles & the endomembrane system
  • How organelles work together
  • Plant vs. animal cell differences

Sort it: prokaryotic vs. eukaryotic

BothProkaryotic onlyEukaryotic only
Cytoplasm, DNA, plasma membrane, ribosomes DNA in nucleoid region, no membrane-bound organelles, no nucleus Membrane-bound organelles, mitochondria, nucleus

Structure ↔ function

  • Produces most cellular ATP → mitochondrion
  • Modifies/packages proteins → Golgi apparatus
  • Protein synthesis → ribosome
  • Lipid synthesis → smooth ER
  • Contains chromosomes → nucleus
  • Ribosome assembly → nucleolus
  • Intracellular digestion/recycling → lysosome
  • Photosynthesis → chloroplast
  • Water storage in plant cells → central vacuole

Worked examples from the study guide

A pancreatic cell produces and secretes large amounts of protein — which structures matter, and in what order?

ribosome → rough ER → Golgi → vesicle → plasma membrane.

A cell produces large quantities of steroid hormones or other lipids — which organelle would be abundant?

Smooth ER.

As a cell gets larger, volume increases faster than surface area — why is that a problem?

Less membrane surface area relative to volume makes exchanging nutrients, gases, and wastes across the plasma membrane less efficient for the cell's needs.

Why do phospholipids naturally form a bilayer in water?

Hydrophilic heads interact with water while hydrophobic tails avoid water and orient inward, away from it.
